# The Publishing Game: How Editors, Reviewers, and Authors Shape Informetric Dynamics

Reference implementation and archived numerical output for:

> J. A. García, R. Rodriguez-Sánchez, J. Fdez-Valdivia,
> **"The Publishing Game: How Editors, Reviewers, and Authors Shape
> Informetric Dynamics"**, *Journal of Informetrics* (under review,
> manuscript JOI-D-26-00430, second revision).

The model is a tripartite sequential game (editor–reviewer–author) whose
equilibrium is computed as a fixed point of the agents' coupled best
responses by damped best-response iteration, and evaluated by Monte Carlo
simulation.

## Requirements

Python 3.9+ with `numpy`, `scipy`, and `matplotlib`
(`pip install -r requirements.txt`).

## How to run

Full recomputation (recreates `results.json` and every PNG figure from
scratch; about 45 minutes on one core, most of it the solver-robustness
block of Appendix E):

```bash
python final_run_v3_6.py
```

Figures only (rebuilds every PNG figure of the paper from the archived
`results.json`, in seconds; `figphase2.png`, Figure 3, re-solves one
configuration at its first replication seed):

```bash
python final_run_v3_6.py --figures-only
```

Both commands read and write `results.json` and the PNG files in the
current directory.

## Reproducibility

* Base seed: 42. Unless noted otherwise, every reported statistic is the
  mean over ten independent replications per configuration, each with
  N = 10,000 manuscripts. The threshold scans (keys `sens_tipping`,
  `sens_bstar_k0`, `sens_bstar_wr`, `sens_bstar_kappa`,
  `sens_bstar_kappa0`, and the fields `beta_star_scan`, `k0_cliff` and
  `w_r_star`) are single fixed-point computations per grid node at the
  base seed with N = 4,000. The fine contract grid (`exp2_WJ`) is solved
  at every grid point on the ten seeds of the threshold contract
  β_r = 0.40 (common random numbers).
* Replication seeds are derived deterministically from the base seed and
  the configuration tuple — replication index, reputational weight (1−φ),
  quality reward β_r, and friction k₀ — via NumPy's `SeedSequence`, so
  every table and figure of the manuscript (including the appendices) is
  reproducible without further input.
* Monte Carlo standard errors are computed across replications and stored
  in `results.json` alongside every headline quantity (`*_se` keys). The
  per-replication values of the efficiency index, of net value per
  manuscript and of the acceptance rate of review invitations are stored
  as `GER_runs`, `NV_runs` and `pi_runs`.
* The eight cells of Experiment IV are additionally run on common random
  numbers (the ten seeds of the Status Quo cell), so that reform contrasts
  are computed as paired differences (`exp4_crn`, `exp4_contrasts`).
* For every replicated configuration of the tables (Experiments I–V,
  controls, factorial, benchmarks, Appendices C–E) the archive records the
  mean and the maximum over replications of the iteration count and of
  the fixed-point residual of the solver, and the share of replications
  that met the tolerance (`n_iter`, `resid_stop`, `resid`, `n_iter_max`,
  `resid_max`, `resid_stop_max`, `converged_share`). The fine contract
  grid (`exp2_WJ`), the single-seed threshold scans and the participation
  profiles carry no diagnostics.
* The archived `results.json` and PNG files were produced with
  Python 3.13, NumPy 2.5.3, SciPy 1.18.1 and Matplotlib 3.11.2. In that
  environment a full run reproduces `results.json` byte for byte; other
  versions may differ in the last floating-point digits, and in the PNG
  files at pixel level.

## What changed from v3.4 (the release cited in the previous version of the manuscript)

1. **Standards.** The fold b = max(s, 1 − s) is removed: the priors are
   defined on [0.5, 1] and b = s; priors outside that range raise a
   `ValueError` (`_solve`, called by `run_config`). No number changes.
2. **Experiment IV is the full 2 × 2 × 2 factorial of reforms**, under both
   institutions, on each cell's own seeds (`exp4`, `exp4_dro`) and on
   common random numbers (`exp4_crn`, `exp4_dro_crn`), with Welch and
   paired contrasts, the conditional effect of each reform in each
   context, and the three-way interaction (`exp4_contrasts`,
   `exp4_dro_contrasts`); contrasts on absolute net value per manuscript
   (`*_contrasts_nv`); benchmarks of the rationed cells; cells that
   separate the level of the authors' standard from the editor–author gap
   (`exp4_levelgap`); the joint package with a flat fee in place of the
   stake (`exp4_flat`).
3. **Convergence diagnostics.** Every fixed-point computation returns the
   iteration count, the stopping statistic, the residual re-evaluated at
   the reported state and a convergence flag (`n_iter`, `resid_stop`,
   `resid`, `converged`); see Reproducibility for what is archived.
4. **Participation-consistent equilibrium.** v3.4 tested the reviewer's
   participation constraint only at the manuscript pool induced by full
   participation and, if it failed, removed the review channel. At the
   pool that a closed channel leaves behind, however, the reviewer's
   maximized utility is positive in almost all of those configurations, so
   declining is not a best response there. v3.6 solves the participation
   stage consistently: a share π of the review invitations is accepted (a
   declined invitation falls back on the desk), and π* satisfies
   Π_r(π*) = 0 whenever neither corner is an equilibrium (`run_config`,
   `participation="equilibrium"`, the default). The v3.4 rule is kept as
   the closed-channel benchmark (`participation="closed"`), and full
   participation can be imposed (`participation=False`).
5. **Experiment I controls.** No reputational motive (`exp1_r0`);
   open-loop decomposition with the manuscript pool and/or the reviewer's
   effort frozen at the healthy baseline (`exp1_frozen`); reputational
   beliefs conditioned on pathway and disposition (`exp1_mu_ta`). The desk
   rate of the low-ability editor and the beliefs are stored for every
   configuration.
6. **Experiment II.** Finer contract grid on common random numbers
   (`exp2_WJ`); closed-channel and forced-participation benchmarks;
   flat-fee benchmark (`exp2_flatfee`: the smallest fixed reward that
   sustains full participation without a quality stake).
7. **Experiment V.** Five friction levels, with both benchmarks.
8. **Sensitivity S3 (new).** Crowding out of the fixed reward by the stake:
   a proportional loss, with the reward floored at zero
   (`sens_bstar_kappa`), and a fixed loss at the introduction of a stake
   (`sens_bstar_kappa0`).
9. **Appendix D.** A fourth structural variant in which the author's
   return depends on the discriminating power of review (`pub_return`).
   The contract at which each variant is evaluated is the grid-scan
   location (`beta_star_scan`) confirmed on the ten replications: it is
   raised by one grid step while the replication-averaged utility of the
   reviewer under full participation is negative (`beta_star`); this
   moves the fourth variant only (0.35 → 0.40).
10. **Appendix E (new).** Every main configuration is re-solved to a
    tolerance of 10⁻⁷ on the same map and on a continuous version of it
    (`convergence`); the participation stage and the Experiment IV
    contrasts of both institutions (`convergence.exp4_contrasts`,
    `convergence.exp4_dro_contrasts`) are re-solved under each
    alternative solver.
11. **Participation profile.** For six rationed configurations the
    reviewer's maximized utility is archived on a grid of imposed
    acceptance rates (`participation_profile`); it is decreasing in the
    acceptance rate in every replication, so the interior root located by
    bisection is unique there.

Of the 3,053 values archived by v3.4, 2,653 are reproduced bit for bit
under the same key (Experiments I and III, the cells of Experiment IV
reported before, the forced-participation benchmarks, the desk-reject-only
runs, and the sensitivity scans S1–S2). The v3.4 values of the
configurations that are now rationed (the penalty-only and β_r = 0.20
contracts of Experiment II; k₀ = 3 and 5 of Experiment V) are reproduced
bit for bit by the closed-channel benchmarks (`exp2_closed`,
`exp5_closed`). The remaining differences are the welfare grid (`exp2_WJ`)
and the structural variants (`structD`), which are now solved with the
participation stage. Earlier releases of the code remain available in the
history of this repository.
